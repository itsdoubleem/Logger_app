근무시간 · Paid on the punch, rounded to the half hour

Rounding is asymmetric, the way companies actually do it. Clock-in rounds up to the next half hour, so being late is never rounded away — 08:40 becomes 09:00, 08:20 becomes 08:30. Punch in genuinely early at 08:30 and it stays 08:30, and that half hour is paid. Clock-out rounds down — 20:15 becomes 20:00, not 20:30 — so no unworked time is ever credited.

The one exception is a 퇴근 유예 grace before the hour, set in 설정 and default 15 minutes: your company credits a 20:45 punch-out as 21:00. Anyone whose company only credits the exact hour sets it to 0. Coming in early is simply more paid time, and turns into overtime once the day passes 8 paid hours.

Open questions for you
소득세 — why it changes every month

It isn't arbitrary. Korean employers withhold from the 국세청 근로소득 간이세액표 (simplified withholding table), keyed to that month's taxable pay and your number of dependents. So a month with heavy 잔업 raises the tax too — that's why yours moved from 91,690 to 169,110 across your 7 payslips. 주민세 is always exactly 10% of 소득세, which your payslips confirm to the won.

The estimate is a progressive approximation of that table — published anchor points plus two verified from your payslips, so it reproduces your 91,690 at 3.08M — with the near-zero floor the real table has below about 1.06M and a 부양가족 수 field, the table's other axis. Outside the anchored range it warns and points at 직접 입력 rather than guessing.

It's also only provisional: February's 연말정산 settles the year and usually refunds over-withholding. So 설정 offers both — 자동 추정 or 직접 입력 of the real figure off the last payslip, which stays the accurate choice for anyone on a different salary.

설정 also flags a sub-minimum hourly: if 기본금 ÷ 기준시간 falls under the 2026 minimum of ₩10,320, it says so. For a worker who doesn't know the rule, that's the single most useful thing this app can tell them.
