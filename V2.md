# Work Log v2 — what I found using it, and what I changed

Written from the seat of an EPS E-9 worker: night shifts, 잔업 every day, a payslip
in a language they half-read, and a phone that holds the only record of any of it.

Files: `WorkLogApp.v2.dc.html` (source) → `python3 build.py v2` → `dist-v2/`.
Wording lives in `lang/*.json`; `python3 tools/sync_lang.py` folds it into the
source. All checks: `sh test/run.sh`.
v1 is untouched and still builds with `python3 build.py`.

---

## Part 1 — Bugs found

Each was reproduced against the v1 code before it was fixed, and each fix has a
regression test in the suite below.

### A. A shift worked a year later silently destroyed last year's

v1 identified a day by `m * 100 + day`. 21 July 2026 and 21 July 2027 are both
`721`. `punch()` replaces any record with a matching key, so the moment a worker
completed a full year, every punch began overwriting the same date from the year
before — no warning, no undo, nothing in the log to notice. For an app whose
stated purpose is "a year of evidence", this quietly ate the evidence.

**Fixed.** Records carry a real year; the key is `y*10000 + m*100 + day`. v1 data
is migrated on first launch by assigning each record the year that puts it
nearest the upgrade date — correct for all v1 data, which could never span more
than 12 months without colliding anyway. The old `worklog.v1` key is left in
place, so nothing is destroyed if a worker rolls back.

### B. The leave balance drifted wrong as soon as the log spanned a year

`annualLeft()` was `annualBase − (every 연차 record in the app, ever)`. Leave taken
last year kept counting against this year's balance. A worker with 9 days left and
two 연차 days logged last March was shown 7.

**Fixed.** The app now stores *when* the balance was stated (`annualAsOf`, set
automatically the moment the field is edited) and counts only leave logged after
that date. The setup screen shows the arithmetic: `08.02 기준 9일 · 그 뒤 사용 1일
→ 남음 8일`.

### C. Being sent home spent a day of the worker's annual leave

The "박스 부족 · 귀가" button wrote `type: 'annual'`. A worker who turned up, was
told there was no work, and went home, lost a day of leave they still had. The
detail line even said it "may qualify for 70% shutdown allowance" — while
recording it as leave anyway.

**Fixed.** It now writes a `shutdown` record. 근로기준법 제46조: an employer-side
shutdown pays at least 70% of average wages. The row reads `휴업 · 일이 없어 귀가 ·
연차 아님` and the balance is untouched. (v1's `sentHome()` also referenced an
undefined `inD` and would have thrown — the button was broken outright.)

### D. No way to record not working — so pay was always overstated

There was no 결근 (absence) and no 휴업 (shutdown). 기본금 was paid in full every
period regardless. A month with an unpaid absence showed more money than the
worker would actually receive. For a wage app that is the one direction that must
never be wrong: a worker who trusts a too-high figure walks into a dispute they
lose.

**Fixed.** Both day types added, with the pay effect shown per day and rolled into
the period total.

### E. Every allowance was taxed and charged insurance

식대 is tax-free up to ₩200,000 a month, and tax-free pay carries no 4대보험
either. v1 had no taxable/non-taxable flag, so a worker with a ₩200,000 meal
allowance was shown too much income tax *and* an inflated 보수월액.

**Fixed.** Each allowance has a 과세/비과세 chip. The exemption is capped at
₩200,000 with the excess returned to taxable, and 보수월액 defaults to the taxable
figure. Warned if a row is marked tax-free above the cap.

### F. The app wrote its whole database to localStorage once a second

The clock tick is a `setState`, and `componentDidUpdate` saved on any change —
about 86,400 full `JSON.stringify` writes a day for a counter that only redraws
`NOW 09:14`. On a cheap Android phone that is real battery and flash wear.

**Fixed.** The payload is serialised first and written only when it differs.
Ten ticks now cause one write instead of ten.

### G. The ×2.0 holiday tier vanished in any year the holiday table didn't know

The built-in holidays cover 2026–2027 only. Work 설날 2028 and `isTrueHoliday()`
returns false, so 근로기준법 제56조② (×2.0 past 8 hours) never applies. v1's own
warning told the worker to "mark it 특근 by hand" — but marking 특근 by hand did
not restore the ×2.0 tier, only the ×1.5. The advice did not do what it said.

**Fixed.** A 특근 day that isn't a known holiday or a Sunday now offers
`법정 휴일로 표시` in its detail. Marking it applies the ×2.0 tier and recalculates.

### H. Blank calendar cells emitted a dangling CSS declaration

Leading blanks lacked `strike`, so the template rendered `text-decoration:` with
no value on every blank cell. Cosmetic, but it was there. Fixed.

## Part 2 — What I'd want as an EPS worker, and what I built

### 1. "Am I actually being paid this?" — 명세서 대조 *(the one that matters)*

v1 computed a beautiful estimate and then left the worker to compare it against a
Korean payslip by eye. That comparison *is* the job. Everything else in the app is
scaffolding for it.

The PAY tab now has a two-column table: what the app counted from the worker's own
punches, and an empty box for what the payslip actually says — 기본금, 연장, 야간,
휴일, 지급총액, 공제총액, 실수령액. Type the payslip in and every line shows its
difference; short lines go red; a banner totals it:

> **덜 받은 것으로 보이는 금액 · ₩125,080**

with a plain-language verdict that is careful about its own limits — tax and
insurance in the app are *estimates*, so a gap there can be normal, but a gap in
basic, overtime, night or holiday pay is not. Stored per pay period, so last
month's comparison survives.

### 2. Something you can actually show someone — 근무내역서

The JSON backup restores the app and proves nothing to anybody. A worker in a
dispute needs one page a Korean employer, a 노무사 or 고용노동부 can read.

New button generates a self-contained printable HTML document, Korean throughout
regardless of the app's language: the rate basis, every day with punches and
hours, the totals, and — if present — the 52-hour breaches and any unpaid holiday
premium, each with its statute cited. It states plainly that tax and insurance are
estimates and the company's payslip governs.

### 3. The 52-hour week — 근로기준법 제50조·제53조

The app already knew every hour. It never once mentioned that six 13-hour days is
69 hours in a week, against a legal ceiling of 52. EPS workers are pushed past
this routinely and it is exactly the thing they cannot easily check themselves.

Weeks now run Monday–Sunday; any week over 52 total or 12 overtime hours raises a
red-bordered banner on both LOGS and PAY, naming the week and the excess, and
saying the part workers most often get told wrong: **being paid for the extra
hours does not make the excess lawful, and agreeing to it does not either.**

### 4. Text that is loud enough to be read

The pattern in v1 was a 10.5px grey note under everything, warnings included. The
things that can cost money now look like it: the 52-hour breach and the underpaid
total are red-bordered blocks with inverted headers, in the same Modernist
vocabulary as the rest — flat, square, 2–3px rules. Nothing new was invented and
no corner was rounded.

### 5. Precision where the worker is asked to trust a number

Every new figure says where it came from and admits when it is an estimate:
결근 shows `시급 × 8h` and warns that companies differ and 주휴수당 may also be lost;
휴업 shows the 70% and cites §46; the leave balance shows its own arithmetic.

### 6. Why the day ended early — 조퇴 사유 *(added after a real shift)*

A mould broke, the parts came out defective, and everyone was sent home at 2pm.
The app recorded `09:00 → 14:00 · 4.0h · ₩0` and threw away the only fact that
made the day mean anything. Those five characters — *the mould broke* — are the
difference between a shutdown the employer owes for under 근로기준법 제46조 and
an ordinary short day the worker eats.

**Punching out short now asks why, before the record is written.** Twelve
built-in reasons in three groups, each carrying a legal classification:

| | |
|---|---|
| 회사 사정 → §46 | 일감 부족 · 자재 부족 · 기계/금형 고장 · 불량 발생 · 정전 · 회사 지시 |
| 본인 사정 | 몸이 아파서 · 병원 진료 · 개인 사정 |
| 그 밖 | 업무 중 부상 (산재) · 천재지변 (§46②) · 직접 입력 |

The reason is not a label, it is a **classifier**. `fault: 'employer'` is what
makes the app compute §46 for that day; the other two make it stay quiet. Picking
`업무 중 부상` says plainly that this is 산재보험, not §46, and to tell the company.

**Built-in beats free text, and the reason is the languages.** A Thai worker who
types `ไม่มีกล่อง` has written something true that nobody in the office can read.
A worker who *picks* 자재 부족 gets `자재 · 부자재 부족` printed on the 근무내역서
whatever language the app is set to. So the list is the main road and the text box
is the shoulder: the note field carries a standing request to write in Korean, and
warns — without ever blocking — when what was typed contains no Hangul. Even then
the picked category and its Korean survive, so the record stays readable.

**When it asks.** Not at eight hours. This factory's day shift is 09:00–18:00 with
two breaks, which is 7.5 net — a fixed 8-hour trigger would fire on a completely
normal day, every day. The threshold is instead the worker's own median for that
shift kind, less an hour: 10.5h normal → asks below 9.5h. Under three samples it
falls back to 6h, low enough never to nag a new install. A day it doesn't catch
can still be tagged by hand from its log row, which is also how the past gets
filled in.

**It never blocks the punch.** The shift is built before the sheet opens and
`사유 없이 퇴근` writes it unchanged. A worker who wants to go home goes home.

#### 6b. …and then it had to stop being a sheet *(2026-08-13, after using 6 on shift)*

The classification in §6 was right. The way it was put on screen was not, and the
first real use found all three failures at once.

**It was two copies of one thing, each wrong in its own way.** The sheet was written
inline into the PUNCH tab and again into the LOGS tab. In LOGS it rendered at the very
top of the tab, so tapping `사유 고치기` on a row far down the list appeared to do
nothing at all. The app did scroll you up — but the screen you landed on had no visible
connection to the button you had just pressed, so it read as the app losing your place
rather than as an answer to what you did. In PUNCH all twelve reasons sat permanently
expanded, which made the sheet longer than the phone and put `사유 저장` off-screen
under the fold.

**And the fastest path to it could not use it.** Clocking out a few minutes after
clocking in never reaches §6 at all — it hits the older `너무 이른 퇴근` guard, whose
one reason-bearing option (`일감 부족 · 귀가`) stamps a reason and closes. A worker
sent home because a mould broke got a day tagged `일감 부족`, with no way to correct it
without going to LOGS afterwards and finding the button described above.

**Now it is one popup, defined once, outside every tab.** Absolutely positioned over
the whole app with a dimmed backdrop, so it arrives where the tap happened and is
plainly *because* of the tap. Tap the dark area to close. Both entry points open the
same element, so there is one thing to look at and one place to change.

The twelve reasons became a **dropdown**: collapsed it shows the reason you picked,
expanding shows all twelve still grouped by fault. Picking collapses it. That is what
brings the save button back onto the screen, which is the whole point of the sheet.

`sentHome()` and `forceClockOut()` now open the popup after writing their record.
`sentHome()` keeps pre-picking `일감 부족` — it remains the likeliest answer, and a
worker who dismisses the popup should be left with a sensible reason rather than none —
but the other eleven are one tap away, on the screen where the day is actually being
recorded.

**A label that had outlived its behaviour.** The button in the 너무 이른 퇴근 guard
still read `박스 부족 · 연차로 기록` / `NO BOXES · SENT HOME → LEAVE`. Part 1 §C stopped
that day costing 연차 a version ago; the label was never updated, so the app was
offering to spend a day of leave and then not doing it. It now reads
`일감 부족 · 휴업으로 기록` / `NO WORK · SENT HOME → SHUTDOWN`. This is exactly the
non-negotiable in `README.md` — every label must match what the code actually does.

#### 6c. The update nobody could see *(found while checking 6b on the phone)*

6b was built, tested, and installed — and the phone still showed the old screen. The
APK was right; the app was not running it.

`pwa/sw.js` served navigations **cache-first**, handing back the stored shell instantly
and fetching the new one for next time. Offline that is exactly right. In the APK it
meant every update was invisible on the launch that followed it, and appeared only on
the one after — so the way to see a new build was to open the app twice, which nobody
knows to do. A worker who installs an update, opens it, and finds nothing changed
concludes the update failed. In an app whose whole claim is that it can be trusted with
wage evidence, teaching that lesson is expensive.

**Fixed.** Navigations race the network against a 1.5-second timer and fall back to the
cache. Inside the APK the page is served by `WebViewAssetLoader` from the device itself,
so the "network" is local and instant and the new build is there the first time. With
the network cut entirely the fetch fails at once and it is cache-first again — measured
at 24ms to open offline against 14ms online, so the offline promise costs nothing.

The cache key was also wrong in a quieter way: `build.py` derived it from `index.html`
alone, so a fix to the worker shipped under the *old* cache name. The browser swaps a
worker whose bytes changed either way, but `activate` then preserves a cache written
under rules that no longer apply. The key is now `sha256(index.html + sw.js)`.

### 7. 휴업수당 was computed on the wrong wage

`shutdownPay()` was `통상임금 × 8 × 70%`. 제46조 says **평균임금** — and 평균임금
includes the last three months' overtime, which for anyone doing 잔업 every day is
well above 통상임금. The app was quietly understating the allowance for exactly the
workers who do the most hours.

Now: 평균임금 from the worker's own records (제2조①6, three months ÷ calendar days),
floored at 통상임금 by 제2조②, capped at 통상임금 by the 제46조① proviso. It can be
typed in from payslips, and when the app's records are under three months it says
so instead of quietly returning a low number. `shutdownPct` — read in two places
and settable in none — is now a real field, for companies that pay 100%.

**Partial shutdowns are covered.** 제46조 is not only for whole days. A day cut
short by the employer where the wage paid falls below the 70% line owes the
difference. Whether it does depends on something the app cannot know — whether the
company deducts the unworked hours — so it states both outcomes rather than
asserting one number:

> 이 날 보장되는 최저 (평균임금 70%) — ₩70,700
> 월급을 그대로 받는다면 — 추가 지급액 없음
> 시간을 깎였다면 (₩41,280) — +₩29,420

**The 근무내역서 gained a 사유 column** and, when there are such days, a §46 table
with the statute quoted, plus a note that 휴업일 are not the worker's 연차 —
제60조⑤ puts the timing of leave in the worker's hands, and 제62조 needs a written
agreement with the 근로자대표.

## Part 3 — Languages

Korean and English are the *second* language of nearly everyone this app is for.
The app now ships in eight: **한국어, English, Tiếng Việt, 中文, ไทย, Bahasa
Indonesia, नेपाली, ភាសាខ្មែរ** — complete, including every statute explanation.

### Korean payslip terms stay visible

A translation that replaces 잔업 with "overtime" leaves the worker unable to find
that line on the piece of paper in their hand, or to name it to a manager. So the
Korean term leads and the translation follows: `잔업 Tăng ca ×1.5`,
`특근 ធ្វើការថ្ងៃឈប់`, `기본금 भित्रै समेटिएको`. The bottom tab bar carries the
worker's language above and the Korean below, so the screen also teaches the word.

The evidence document (`근무내역서`) stays entirely Korean whatever the app is set
to — it exists to be read by a Korean employer or inspector.

### How it is built

`this.t(ko, en)` at 416 call sites became `this.T(key, params)` against a keyed
table. Interpolated strings became named placeholders, so word order is free:
Korean puts the year first (`{y}년 {m}월`), English puts the month first
(`{name} {y}`), and each language decides for itself.

- `lang/base.json` — 504 keys, ko + en, the reference wording
- `lang/<code>.json` — one language, same keys
- `tools/sync_lang.py` — folds them into `Component.STR` in the app source
- `tools/check_lang.py` — validates before they ship

**Adding a language is adding a file.** No code changes: drop `lang/tl.json` in,
add one row to `Component.LANGS`, run sync. Anything a file does not translate
falls back to English, then Korean — a screen never comes up blank.

27 of the 504 keys are deliberately never translated: the English gloss printed
under a translated heading, and the Korean payslip words used as secondary lines.
`check_lang.py` knows the difference and does not report them missing.

### What the checker enforces

Not spelling — the things that break a translated build: unknown keys (a typo
means that string silently never appears), invented placeholders (`{p9}` would
render literally on screen), and `|` lists that changed length (weekday and month
names are read by index, so a missing item is an undefined day).

### Digits

Nepali and Khmer prose uses ASCII digits, not Devanagari or Khmer numerals. Every
figure the app *computes* is ASCII, and so is every figure on a Korean payslip —
mixing scripts inside one sentence would make the number the worker is trying to
check harder to find, not easier.

## Not done, and why

**The other eight EPS languages.** Tagalog, Burmese, Sinhala, Bengali, Urdu,
Mongolian, Uzbek, Lao and Tetum are not here. The machinery is: each is one JSON
file against `lang/base.json`, validated by `check_lang.py`.

**Holidays past 2027.** I did not hardcode 2028+ lunar dates. 설날 and 추석 move
each year and I am not confident enough in those dates to put them where a wrong
one becomes wrong pay. The hand-marking fix (G) is the honest answer: the worker
marks the day, the ×2.0 applies, and it no longer depends on the table being
current.

**결근 deduction method.** Companies calculate it differently (시급 × 8h vs
기본금 ÷ 30). It is labelled an estimate rather than guessed silently.

## Verification

Everything below runs from `sh test/run.sh`.

- **134 regression tests**, `test/regress.js` — including a direct v1-vs-v2
  comparison proving the wage engine is byte-identical: `calc()` across six shift
  shapes, `snapIn`/`snapOut` across all 96 half-hours, `detectShift` across the
  clock, and all four rates. The engine verified against real payslips was not
  touched.
- **조퇴 사유** — the adaptive threshold (7.5h normal day not flagged, 4h flagged
  with no history, 8.5h flagged once the median is 10.5h, night counted apart),
  the punch never blocked, the classifier surviving a Thai note, backfilling and
  re-tagging a past day, `sentHome()` stamping its own reason, and the note field
  taking the change event rather than storing `[object Object]` — which is what it
  did on the phone before the on-device pass caught it.
- **조퇴 사유 팝업 (§6b)** — the popup opening collapsed with a placeholder and a
  dead save button, expanding to all twelve, collapsing again on a pick with the
  choice held, closing tidying both; `sentHome()` opening it with `일감 부족`
  pre-picked and a re-tag to `기계 · 금형 고장` replacing it without disturbing the
  휴업 record; a dismissed popup leaving `일감 부족` rather than nothing; and
  `forceClockOut()` opening it with the review flag intact.
- **근로기준법 제46조** — 평균임금 floored by 제2조② and capped by the 제46조①
  proviso, the manual figure winning, the thin-records warning, and partial-day
  shortfall arithmetic (₩70,700 floor − ₩41,280 worked = ₩29,420) with 본인 사정
  and untagged days correctly producing nothing.
- **Template binding check** — all 516 `{{ }}` bindings resolve; every `sc-for`
  row field exists on its list's rows.
- **i18n refactor proof** — `test/snapshot.js` renders every string the app can
  produce across 38 screen states (idle, on-shift, overrun, each pending sheet,
  each expanded log row, manual insurance, day-only, low wage, unknown year, each
  biometric state…): 42,065 strings per language. Captured before the refactor and
  diffed after. **Korean is byte-identical.** English differs in exactly one place,
  where the new wording fixes an ungrammatical "1 vacation days" → "1 day
  vacation". The only other changes are the language picker itself and its new
  close button.
- **Every language renders** — all eight produce 42,065 strings with zero throws
  and zero unresolved `{placeholders}`.
- **Translation validation** — `tools/check_lang.py`: 540/540 in each of the six
  new languages, no unknown keys, no invented placeholders, no broken lists.
- **Headless Chrome** — boots with zero console errors; first-run setup, all four
  tabs, the new day types, the 52-hour banner and the payslip comparison driven
  end to end (₩325,080 computed → ₩200,000 entered → ₩125,080 shortfall shown and
  persisted → corrected → banner clears).
- **Upgrade path** — a v1 payload planted in localStorage loads into v2 with years
  assigned, removed-keys re-keyed, setup not re-shown, and `worklog.v1` preserved.

## Unchanged on purpose

No server, no account, no telemetry — the translations ship inside the file and
nothing is fetched at runtime. The wage engine, the Modernist design system,
WebAuthn punch with press-and-hold fallback, and the asymmetric punch rounding are
all exactly as they were.
